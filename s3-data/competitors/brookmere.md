# Brookmere — Competitor Profile

> **NOTICE:** The content in this document is illustrative and represents a fictional competitor used for demo and training purposes. It does not represent any real company. Advisors must not quote this content as factual information about real insurers in any customer-facing conversation.

## Archetype summary

Brookmere represents the direct-to-consumer, price-led online insurer. Policies are purchased entirely through a mobile app or website, with no human advisor in the buying journey and no agent network. The brand competes primarily on headline price, typically positioned 20-30% below the market average for its targeted lines, and the product portfolio is deliberately narrow. Pricing tier: **budget**.

Typical Brookmere customers are price-sensitive, digital-native, and comfortable self-serving their insurance needs. They are frequently first-time policyholders, younger renters, or customers who only need the minimum coverage required by law or by a lender.

## Known strengths

From an insurance-professional perspective, Brookmere's archetype has genuine strengths that advisors should acknowledge when positioning against it:

- **Price competitiveness.** Headline premiums are deliberately positioned below market average, with efficient online-only distribution keeping acquisition costs low.
- **Speed of purchase.** The quote-to-bind journey is streamlined to a few minutes, which appeals to customers who want coverage active the same day.
- **Simplicity of product design.** Limited customisation reduces decision fatigue for customers who are not sure what they need and want a clear default.
- **Mobile-first experience.** The app is designed for one-device end-to-end management: quoting, binding, policy documents, claims, and cancellation.
- **Transparent pricing.** Customers see the full price before any personal information is collected, which builds trust with price-sensitive segments.
- **Low-friction cancellation.** Monthly payment and easy cancellation in-app suit short-tenure customers and those with changing circumstances.

## Known weaknesses

These are factual gaps to reference when a prospect raises specific concerns. Frame them as structural trade-offs of the budget model, not as failures.

- **No human advisor in the buying journey.** Customers select coverage themselves, with no advisor recommendation. For customers who are not confident in assessing their own coverage needs, this can lead to under-insurance.
- **Narrow product portfolio.** Six lines are offered (auto, renters, simplified term life, simplified whole life, travel, simplified critical illness). There is no health insurance, no home insurance for owner-occupiers, and no disability line. There is no universal or variable life product.
- **Limited coverage depth.** Coverage caps and optional riders are fewer than on mid-market or premium products. High-value property, specialised liability, or complex life insurance needs are not served.
- **Simplified underwriting trade-offs.** Simplified-issue life insurance comes with lower coverage caps and, in some cases, higher per-dollar premium than fully underwritten policies for healthy applicants.
- **No bundling discounts.** With a narrow portfolio and no cross-sell strategy, Brookmere does not offer multi-policy discounts.
- **Claims support.** Simple claims are handled through an in-app flow quickly, but complex or disputed claims have limited escalation paths and no in-person adjuster network.
- **No 24/7 live claims hotline.** Customer support is primarily asynchronous (in-app chat or email). There is no phone line staffed around the clock for high-stress events.
- **Coverage gaps for life events.** As customers' lives become more complex (buying a home, having children, taking out a mortgage, starting a business), the Brookmere portfolio stops mapping to their needs.

## What Awesome Insurance offers that Brookmere does not

When a prospect is comparing Awesome Insurance to a Brookmere-style budget insurer, lead with coverage depth and life-stage fit:

- **Broader product lines.** Awesome covers Awesome Term Life, Awesome Health, Awesome Motor, Awesome Home Insurance, Awesome Disability, Awesome Travel, and Awesome Critical Illness. Brookmere offers a simplified critical illness product (BasicCover Shield) with limited face amounts and a narrow covered-conditions list, but does not offer health insurance, home insurance for owner-occupiers, or disability coverage at all.
- **Full life insurance type coverage.** Awesome offers all four primary life insurance structures — term (Awesome Term Life, up to $2M), whole life (Awesome Whole Life, up to $2.5M), universal life (Awesome Universal Life, up to $5M), and variable life (Awesome Variable Life, up to $10M). Brookmere offers only simplified-issue term (up to ~$250K) and a small-face simplified whole life (up to ~$50K) aimed at final-expense coverage. Customers needing meaningful permanent coverage, flexible premium universal life, or investment-linked variable life are structurally unserved by Brookmere's portfolio.
- **Higher coverage caps on shared lines.** Awesome's Awesome Term Life offers coverage up to $2,000,000 compared to Brookmere's approximate $250,000 cap on simplified-issue term, and Awesome Whole Life reaches $2.5M versus BasicCover Forever's $50K ceiling. Awesome Health offers annual benefit limits up to $250,000 where Brookmere does not offer a health product. Awesome Home covers properties worth $2,000,000+ where Brookmere does not serve owner-occupiers at all.
- **Configurable products.** Awesome's customer-selectable features (coverage tier, deductible, term, reimbursement model, hospital network, liability limits) give customers a tailored policy, versus Brookmere's fixed-tier approach.
- **Optional riders across lines.** Awesome's products include accelerated death benefit, waiver of premium, recovery care, chronic condition management, flood protection, new vehicle replacement, roadside assistance, adventure sports coverage, cost-of-living adjustments, and future purchase options. Brookmere's budget positioning excludes most of these.
- **Human advisor access.** Awesome advisors are available before, during, and after purchase — Brookmere's model has no human advisor at all. For customers making complex coverage decisions (determining the right life insurance amount for a young family, comparing disability coverage tiers for a self-employed professional), advisor access materially changes the outcome.
- **Multi-policy bundling.** Customers combining motor, home, life, and health with Awesome access bundle discounts that Brookmere structurally cannot offer.
- **Claims depth.** Awesome supports both fast in-app claims for simple events and escalation paths for complex events, including 24/7 claims support. Brookmere's asynchronous support model is not designed for high-complexity claims.
- **Deeper critical illness and dedicated disability lines.** Awesome Critical Illness offers lump-sum payments from $25,000 to $500,000 across three tiered covered-conditions categories (Basic, Standard, Comprehensive) and supports optional Recovery Care Benefit and Child Critical Illness Coverage. Brookmere's BasicCover Shield caps at approximately $50,000 with a narrow covered-conditions list (cancer, heart attack, stroke only) and no optional riders. Awesome Disability replaces monthly income if the policyholder cannot work; Brookmere does not offer disability coverage at all.
- **Travel insurance depth.** Awesome Travel offers geographic coverage up to Worldwide, medical coverage up to unlimited, annual multi-trip plans, and adventure sports riders. BasicCover Travel is a single-trip-only product with a fixed medical cap.

## Positioning guidance for advisors

When a prospect is using a Brookmere-style budget insurer, the most effective conversation is one that explores what the customer's life currently looks like and what it is likely to look like in three to five years. Customers who only need minimum-required auto liability or basic renters coverage may be well-served by a budget insurer today, and advisors should acknowledge that honestly. The stronger Awesome case appears at life-stage transitions — buying a home, starting a family, going self-employed, planning international travel, or recognising critical illness or disability exposure — where the Brookmere portfolio no longer covers the customer's situation. Frame the move to Awesome as a match to a broader set of needs, not as a price comparison.
